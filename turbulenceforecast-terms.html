2. What Turbulence Forecast does

Turbulence Forecast estimates how bumpy a flight is likely to feel, and when. It builds a route from the two airports, fetches public weather model data and official aviation warnings for that route and time, and computes a forecast on your device. It shows you which stretches may be rough, how rough, for roughly how long, and how confident the estimate is.

A forecast is an estimate, not a promise. Weather models are updated several times a day and disagree with each other; routes change; aircraft type and loading change what you feel. Every figure Turbulence Forecast shows carries its source and the time it was valid, so you can judge it for yourself.

7. Data sources and accuracy

Turbulence Forecast uses public weather model data from Open-Meteo, and official warnings and observations from the US National Weather Service’s aviation weather service. We do not control those services, their availability or the accuracy of what they publish. When a source is late, unavailable or over its quota, Turbulence Forecast tells you rather than showing you a stale figure as if it were fresh.

Coverage is not uniform. Pilot reports are dense over the United States and sparse elsewhere; some regions publish no public airspace boundaries at all. The app marks what it does not know instead of filling the gap.
